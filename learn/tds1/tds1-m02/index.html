TestSheets and Attributes

TestSheet

A TestSheet organizes logical test data. The documentation recommends covering each Requirement with a TestSheet; creating a sheet alone does not prove its generated tests fully cover the criterion.

Attribute

A characteristic or business object involved in the behavior being tested — for example, a credit-card drop-down on a checkout form. Attributes describe *what varies*; the specific values an Attribute can take (e.g. Visa, Mastercard) are added later as Instances (Lesson 3).

Structuring Attributes

Like Requirements, Attributes can be layered — every Attribute may have sub-Attributes, depending on how much detail the TestSheet needs.

Nested structure versus business relevance

Two different concerns

Attributes can contain sub-Attributes and Instances. BusinessRelevant separately identifies input relevance or verification data. The accessed 2026.1 pages do not establish the earlier lesson’s claim about a non-editable AttrType property; do not rely on that unverified UI rule.

The four core Attributes

Recommended structure

Independent Quilyn organizational example: group metadata, setup, inputs and expected results under four named Attributes when that makes a TestSheet easier to understand. These exact names are not a mandatory or verified universal Tricentis recommendation.

BusinessRelevant property

Three possible values

BusinessRelevant is user-controlled. Yes is the default; No identifies non-relevant data and Result identifies verification data. Changing the parent setting also changes its sub-Attributes.

Setting BusinessRelevant on a parent Attribute propagates that value down to all of its sub-Attributes.

Building it in Tosca

Toggle Business Relevance in the ribbon or with Ctrl+F7. Inspect the resulting BusinessRelevant value and descendants; the icon color also indicates relevance.
