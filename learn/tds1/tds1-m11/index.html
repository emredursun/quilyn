Worked example: a Loyalty Tier discount

Business context

Independent assumptions: a checkout grants Guest 0%, Bronze 5%, Silver 10% and Gold 15% discounts on a subtotal of 100, before tax and shipping. An expired loyalty account is rejected. Assume Guest is the most frequent, least-dependent checkout path and a failure there has the greatest business impact. These are exercise assumptions, not facts about a real webshop.

Step 1 — Requirement

Create a Requirement for loyalty-discount behavior and assess its risk using project evidence. The exercise does not determine a universal Frequency or Damage class from the feature name. Keep expected amounts and rejection behavior traceable to the specified business rules.

Step 2 — Attributes

Use an optional layout with Administration (No), Precondition (Yes), Process (Yes) and Verification (Result). Model Loyalty Tier as the only varying business input; keep other inputs fixed for this exercise. Verification holds the expected amounts or rejection outcome.

Step 3 — Instances

Create Guest as StraightThrough under the stated frequency, dependency and impact assumptions; Bronze, Silver and Gold as Valid; Expired Account as Invalid. For subtotal 100, verify totals 100, 95, 90 and 85 respectively; for Expired Account verify the specified rejection and absence of a completed discounted order. Invalid data passes a test only when all relevant assertions hold.

Step 4 — Combinatorial method

With exactly one varying Attribute containing five choices and no constraints, Linear Expansion produces five datasets: one baseline and four alternatives. All Combinations also produces five in this single-Attribute case; it does not create an unnecessary larger suite here. Neither generation operation executes the tests.

Step 5 — Reuse and linking

A Class is useful if centralized sharing benefits other designs; a mandatory second-use threshold is not established here. Link the five design Instances to the Requirement as substitutes, replace them explicitly after linking physical tests, and inspect execution evidence and recalculated coverage separately.
