Building the Requirements structure

Requirement

A criterion the system under test is expected to fulfil — functional (e.g. 'a customer can pay with a Visa card') or non-functional (e.g. system security). Requirements are gathered from workshops with stakeholders, or from existing specs/BA documents.

Structuring principle

Start from the highest-level functionality (e.g. Customer, Products, Order Process) and expand downward into sub-Requirements. Keep the tree shallow, non-redundant, and easy to read — depth for its own sake makes the structure harder to navigate, not more precise.

Requirement / Sub-Requirement is created via 'Create Object' in the Ribbon, 'Create Requirement' in the Mini-Toolbar, or the shortcut Ctrl+N, Ctrl+R. 'Create Requirement (after this)' (Ctrl+,) instead adds a sibling at the same level as the currently selected Requirement.

Basic Weighting

Weight column

Every Requirement defaults to a Weight of 1 (equal importance). Basic Weighting lets you assign any value from 0-10 per Requirement; Tricentis' recommended best practice is to keep it simple and use a 1-5 scale.

Top-down entry order

Collapse all sub-Requirements first and weight the highest level — this tells you which broad area to start testing in. Then expand each Requirement and weight its Leaf Requirements — this tells you exactly which specific item to focus on inside that area.

Complex Weighting

Why complex weighting exists

A single linear scale often can't distinguish importance clearly enough (is this a 3 or a 4?). Complex Weighting instead asks two separate business questions per Requirement and lets Tosca calculate the Weight for you.

The formula

Weight = 2^(Frequency Class) × 2^(Damage Class). Both inputs are entered on a 0-10 scale (best practice: 1-5), and Tosca calculates the resulting Weight automatically — though it can be overridden manually. Because the formula is exponential (base 2 by default, adjustable on the RequirementSet's Properties tab), Requirements spread out more distinctly than a simple additive or linear scale would allow, which makes prioritization decisions clearer.

As with Basic Weighting, weight the highest-level Requirements first, then expand and weight the Leaf Requirements.

Using Requirements as a dashboard

Contribution (%)

Shows a Requirement's share of risk coverage across the whole RequirementSet. Values are multiplied by their parent's weight going up the tree, so Contribution (%) reflects how much this Requirement matters to the project as a whole — not just to its immediate siblings.

Relative Weight (%)

Shows a Requirement's weight compared only to its siblings on the same level. Relative Weight values for elements on the same level always sum to 100%.

Downstream links

Once weighted, Requirements link outward: TestSheets (TestCase Design) link to Requirements, TestCases link to Requirements, and ExecutionLists link to whole RequirementSets. The ExecutionState column then reflects executed-TestCase results weighted by risk — a risk-based coverage statement, usable as a management report.

Keeping Requirements up to date

Auto-update behavior differs by Tosca version

In Tosca 10.2 and earlier, Requirements recalculate automatically the instant a linked object changes. In Tosca 10.3 and later, auto-update is OFF by default — recalculating on every change can be slow in large repositories with many users and objects.

Manual refresh

When a Requirement's data is stale, an exclamation mark appears next to the affected RequirementSet. Select it and press F6, or click 'Update Outdated Values' in the Ribbon, to recalculate.

To switch auto-update back on in 10.3+: Project >> Settings >> Commander >> General >> Advanced >> 'AutoCalculateRequirements' = On. Worth enabling on smaller projects where recalculation cost isn't a concern.
