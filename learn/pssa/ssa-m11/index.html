Organization records

Choose the right mechanism

Organization, division and unit describe the enterprise hierarchy; units can have children. Work groups represent operational teams that can span business units. These records support routing and reporting but do not replace access roles.

Worked example: A retailer has an organization, regional divisions and fulfillment units. A returns work group spans fulfillment and support without changing the reporting hierarchy.

Configuring Work Groups and Work Queues

Choose the right mechanism

A work queue holds shared assignments; a work group associates operators, managers and queues. A worklist holds assignments for an individual operator. Match team-level routing to a queue and individual ownership to a worklist.

Worked example: Route unclaimed returns reviews to ReturnsQueue, then let eligible operators obtain work. A named specialist's follow-up can go to that specialist's worklist.

Additional tasks for organizational records

Choose the right mechanism

Maintain operator organizational membership, work group/queue associations and routing references together. Test that eligible team members can retrieve shared work and that managers can oversee the intended group; renaming an organization is not a substitute for updating routing.

Worked example: When a new branch opens, create its unit and operators, associate them with the appropriate work group, and test queue access and escalation to the manager.
