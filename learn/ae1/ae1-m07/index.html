The problem: widgets that aren't standard controls

When an engine sees the wrong thing

Standard identification works because the engine recognises a control as something it understands — a text box, a checkbox, a table. Complex or third-party UI widgets break that assumption. A custom date picker, a rich grid, or a bespoke component may be built from dozens of nested primitive elements, so the engine sees a pile of divs or panels rather than one meaningful control. You can sometimes still automate it, but the steering is fragile and the Module is unreadable.

Custom controls

The original theory lesson describes C# customizations that help Tosca recognize or steer nonstandard controls, or perform special execution tasks that built-in engines cannot handle easily. The exercise demonstrates two DLL extensions: a customized combo box and a DIV-based table. Their supported operations depend on the extension, target and product version.

Adapting a widget can preserve the familiar TestStep workflow. It does not guarantee support for all ActionModes, nor immunity to changes in the application.

Where custom controls come from

Compiled extension packages

AE1 Lesson 06 supplies FancyComboBox.dll and HtmlTable.dll as separate attachments. A compiled extension is distinct from a scanned Module or a TestCase object. Obtain the extension and deployment instructions for the actual product and version; the exercise does not teach DLL development inside Commander.

On-prem deployment in the original exercise

The Tosca 16.0 Academy exercise closes Tosca, places the extracted DLL files in C:\Program Files (x86)\TRICENTIS\Tosca Testsuite\TBox, and reopens Commander before rescanning. This is the documented training-installation path, not a universal path for other installations or future versions. Confirm the installed product’s extension instructions.

Validate the extension’s product/version compatibility and deployment on execution environments. Checking test objects into a repository is not evidence that their external dependencies are available on every agent.

Tosca Cloud uses a different route: an administrator uploads via Configurations → Uploads → Custom controls and Launcher distributes the files. That procedure does not affect on-prem customizations and is not the AE1 installation exercise.

The original Tosca 16.0 lesson warns that customizations can interfere with recognition of other control types. Its DLLs are not automatically upgraded with Tosca; recompilation is required for a Tosca version upgrade and may also be needed when the system under test changes. Follow applicable extension guidance and regression-test affected controls.

Using them and where they fit

Validate after deployment

With a compatible adapter, scan and check the recognized control and supported operations. Continue through the ordinary Module/TestStep workflow only after confirming that the adapter supports the required actions.

Structural adaptation can reduce reliance on appearance, but its resilience depends on the widget and adapter. The sequence above is independent guidance, not a verified Academy rule or guarantee that any cosmetic change is safe.

The exercise suggests customizations when supported TBox steering or a workaround cannot handle a control. It also suggests considering Vision AI as a possible alternative to custom development. Neither the exercise nor the independent troubleshooting outline guarantees recognition for every target.

The original learning-objective checklist is a three-item self-assessment: describe custom controls, give a control-customization example and explain important control facts. It is not a scored multiple-choice quiz or proof that the DLLs ran successfully.
