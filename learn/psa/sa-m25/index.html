Processes and Process diagrams

Process

A series of Steps expected to occur in a logical, sequential order. When you add a Process to a Case Type in the Case Life Cycle designer, the Steps are configured in the typical sequence to completion.

Decision shapes in a process flow are like railroad switches: when a train (case) arrives at a switch point, the switch (decision) checks the rail conditions (data values) and automatically routes the train down track A or track B — no driver needed, no delay. The correct track is always taken as long as the conditions are correctly configured.

Process diagram

Pega's visual canvas for advanced flow control. Shapes include Assignments, Automations, Decisions, and Subprocesses. Use the Process diagram when the Case Life Cycle's stage/step view doesn't support the required branching or parallel logic.

Accessing the Process diagram

Click the CONFIGURE PROCESS icon located on the Process in the Case Life Cycle. This opens the PROCESS MODELER, where you add, edit, and remove shapes in the Process diagram to build configurations that deviate from the logical sequential order.

Decision shapes

Decision shape (orange diamond)

Orange diamond that adds conditional branching or skipping to a Process. Advances the Case automatically based on one or more conditions — no user action required at this shape. Supports more than one outcome path.

Automating vs human expertise

Decision automation frees operators to focus on complex cases:

  • AUTOMATE — straightforward decisions with clear rules (e.g., credit score below threshold → auto-deny)
  • HUMAN expertise — ambiguous cases needing judgment (e.g., low credit score but high salary and strong history → agent review)

Decision flow variations

(1) Cases move forward but may include ANOTHER STEP as needed — you choose when extra processing supports the Case. (2) Cases go to ANOTHER STAGE using the Change Stage shape; by skipping a Stage you support out-of-sequence processing.

Flow Actions

Flow Action

Defines what a user can do at a specific Assignment Step — which form appears, which fields are shown, and what submit options are available. Controls user interaction within a Step, not the path between Steps.

Associating a Flow Action

Within a Process, you associate a Flow Action with a CONNECTOR (the arrow between shapes) or an ASSIGNMENT shape (a green rectangle in the Process diagram).

Connector Action

Completes the Assignment AND advances the Case to the next shape. A Connector Action is configured as a LINE shape (the connector) in the Process Modeler.

Local Action

Updates the Assignment but does NOT advance the Case to the next shape. A Local Action is configured in the PROPERTIES PANE of an Assignment shape in the Process Modeler.
