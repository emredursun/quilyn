Traditional Software Architecture (and its problems)

Top-down approach

Designs applications around the PRESENTATION layer's delivery Channels (web, mobile, digital messaging, voice-AI). Its most significant disadvantage: the same business logic must be implemented MULTIPLE TIMES to deliver a consistent experience across the different Channels.

Bottom-up approach

Designs applications around the DATA access layer. Risk: each system has its own data view; legacy systems built in silos duplicate data, and no single solution provides a complete view of a business entity like a Customer.

Roof-first vs. pipes-first vs. core-first: Top-down builds from the roof (Channels) — every new roof re-runs the plumbing (logic). Bottom-up starts from foundation pipes (data) — every room gets a different blueprint. Center-out starts from the living core (business logic) and connects up to roofs and down to pipes, so neither can break the home's logic.

Pega's Center-out Approach

Center-out business architecture

Puts the BUSINESS LOGIC layer at the center, with customer outcome at the core. Presentation and data-access layers stay SEPARATE from business logic — so Channels and databases can change without touching the logic.

Presentation layer (connects UP)

The business logic is connected up into the presentation layer so delivery Channels dynamically RESPOND to changes in the central business logic — no recoding per channel.

Application & business logic layer (the hub)

The central hub containing all the business logic needed to achieve the desired strategic outcome for the organization.

Data access layer (connects DOWN)

Connections down to data are insulated from the business logic, so changes in systems of record — for example, moving from on-premise to Cloud storage — do NOT require changes in the application's business logic.

Hub-and-spoke wheel: business logic is the hub; Channels and data systems are spokes. Replace any spoke (a new mobile channel, a migrated database) and the hub keeps the wheel true — the logic never has to be rebuilt.

The Five Center-out Principles (I-O-U-D-V)

1. Manage intelligence centrally

Center-out principle 1 — start with the business logic:

  • Business outcomes guided by real-time customer information
  • Rules consistently enforced across all channels
  • Everyone uses the SAME intelligence (single source of truth)

2. Focus on outcomes, align your process

Get the work done using Case Management and a MICROJOURNEY approach — implementing a part of the customer journey that is tied to a specific outcome.

3. Connect experiences up to your Channels

Provide a consistent user experience by keeping front-end and back-end logic coordinated; changes are reflected dynamically without recoding.

4. Connect down to your data, keep logic nimble

Access data without adding complexity — quickly and easily define the data needed and access it in running applications without worrying about how or where the data is stored or accessed.

5. Manage variations to be scale-ready

Start small and fast, then scale big and broad and stay future-proof by adapting to different customer types, lines of business, and geographies using Pega's SITUATIONAL LAYER CAKE.

Memory hook 'I-O-U-D-V': Intelligence central, Outcomes/process, Up to Channels, Down to data, Variations to scale — all organized around the customer and the strategic outcome.

Benefits of Center-out Architecture

Efficiency

Developers define business rules ONCE in a central location, eliminating duplication and reducing inconsistencies across channels and applications.

Agility

When business requirements change, you update the CENTRAL logic and the system applies those changes across all channel interfaces — making applications more responsive to evolving needs.

Governance

Centralized business logic means rules are defined once and enforced consistently across all channels — simplifying auditing, monitoring, and policy enforcement.

Scalability

As the organization adds new channel interfaces or customer touchpoints, you REUSE existing business logic to maintain consistency.

Model-driven UI and Patterns

Model-driven UI

An approach in which the underlying data models and business rules GENERATE the user interface. In Center-out, these models reside at the core and the system dynamically generates the UI from them.

Patterns

Reusable templates for model-driven UIs. Define the Data Model and business rules at the center, then SELECT and CONFIGURE patterns to render the interface — maintaining consistency and keeping business logic centralized across devices and channels.

Separation of logic and presentation

A key advantage of patterns: when business rules change, the UI ADAPTS AUTOMATICALLY without manual updates. Pattern-based UIs also support responsive design across devices and channels.

Patterns are cookie cutters: define the dough once (data model + rules at the center), then stamp consistent cookies (forms, lists, dashboards) across every channel. Change the dough recipe and every future cookie updates automatically.

Accessibility in Center-out Development

Centralized accessibility

By centralizing UI generation and using standardized patterns, accessibility requirements implemented ONCE at the core propagate throughout the application — consistent compliance across all channels and touchpoints, with reduced effort and simpler governance.

Standards supported

The architecture supports compliance with major standards including Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 and Section 508 requirements, embedded into the core patterns and components.

Evolving standards

When accessibility standards evolve, updates made to the central components and patterns automatically bring all applications into compliance — without changing each individual screen or form.

Accessibility as building code in the master blueprint: instead of inspecting every room separately, embed the code in the blueprint — every room built from it is compliant, and updating the blueprint updates every room at once.
