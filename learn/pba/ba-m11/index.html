Assignment Routing

Assignment routing

Routing assigns responsibility to configured users or queues using explicit destinations or conditions. Get Next Work separately selects suitable work using urgency, availability and skills; not every routing setting automatically computes the best available agent.

Worklist vs. Work Queue

Two types of Assignment containers:

  • WORKLIST — Assignments routed to a SPECIFIC user (operator) to work individually
  • WORK QUEUE — a SHARED queue for a team/Work Group; any qualified member can pull and complete the work

Routing options

App Studio supports current user, specific user, Work Queue and business-logic routing. Business conditions can choose different user/queue destinations; Get Next Work selection also evaluates urgency, availability and skills.

A Worklist is your personal inbox (addressed to you); a Work Queue is a shared team inbox (anyone on the team can grab the next item). Routing is the mailroom deciding which inbox each task goes to.

Cross-functional Work Groups

Work Group

The sourced Team model describes a primary manager, a shared Work Queue and multiple users, potentially across departments. A user can belong to multiple Work Groups. Authorized managers may transfer work but cannot perform approvals; those outside the group cannot complete its queue tasks.

A Work Group is a project team roster; a cross-functional Work Group is a task force pulled from multiple departments so the whole Case can be handled without hand-off delays.

Approving Work

Approval Step

An Approve/Reject Step routes to a configured user, queue or business condition and applies configured outcome behavior. Approval may continue/change Stage; rejection may resolve, continue or change Stage. Default rejection is Resolved-Rejected, not necessarily rework.

Single vs. Cascading approval

Two approval scopes:

  • SINGLE — one approver only
  • CASCADING — MULTIPLE approvers in sequence (e.g., up a reporting hierarchy or via an authority matrix for higher amounts)

Single approval is one signature on the form; cascading approval is a signature chain that climbs the org chart until the amount is fully authorized.

Managing Work Deadlines: SLAs and Urgency

Service-Level Agreement (SLA)

An SLA defines timing, urgency adjustments, notifications and configured escalations for Case/Stage/Process/Step. Goal and Deadline are measured from the start; repeatable Passed Deadline intervals are measured from the Deadline and configured in Dev Studio.

Goal vs. Deadline

Two SLA time thresholds on an Assignment or Case:

  • GOAL — the EARLIER target; work SHOULD be completed by this time
  • DEADLINE — the LATER limit; work MUST be completed by this time

Case Urgency

Case and Assignment urgency are distinct priorities from 0 to 100. Positive configured increments can raise the relevant urgency at SLA milestones, but do not guarantee completion. At 100 further increments are ignored while configured notifications/escalations still run.

Think of urgency as priority rather than automatic completion: attention and escalation still require the configured workflow and available capacity.
