Who the Business Architect Is

Pega Business Architect (BA)

The liaison who bridges the client organization and the Pega delivery team, understanding business needs and Pega capabilities to produce software that exceeds what either side could build alone.

Member of BOTH teams

On a Pega project, the BA belongs to both the Business team (process and organizational needs) and the IT team (software development and Pega capabilities), bridging the two groups.

Client vs. Customer (terminology)

Two distinct stakeholder roles in Pega implementations:

  • CLIENT ('organization') — the BUSINESS transforming its workflows with Pega
  • CUSTOMER — that business's END CUSTOMER (the person using the final application)

The Bilingual Diplomat: A BA stands between two delegations that speak different languages — Business (customer outcomes and process pain) and IT (Rules, Cases, and Pega capabilities). The BA translates both ways so the treaty they sign — the application — truly serves everyone.

The Four Core Responsibilities (A-P-A-E)

Analysis

Gather a complete picture of problems before configuring a solution:

  • Understand critical stages
  • Identify bottlenecks and workarounds
  • Diagnose root causes
  • Find functionality common across processes

Process Design

Optimize the workflow using Pega GenAI Blueprint:

  • Remove unnecessary steps
  • Re-sequence stages
  • Automate repetitive activities
  • Intelligently allocate work
  • Consolidate data for users

Alignment

Collaborate with Business and IT to design, build, and test the solution using Blueprint and App Studio, ensuring both sides understand requirements and the proposed workflow improvements.

Education

Continuously teach Business stakeholders the value of Pega's out-of-the-box features, helping them focus on outcomes rather than current methods and become advocates for the new process.

A relay race where one runner never stops: Analysis hands to Process Design, which hands to Alignment, which hands to Education — but Education keeps lapping the track the whole project, continually enabling stakeholders to change how they work.

Partnership in Action (worked example)

Sara Han, a Pega BA on a complaints-management project, is asked to review the process so the business resolves concerns faster. She EDUCATES stakeholders on OOTB features (e.g., automatically routing work to the right users with business rules; distributing approval notifications by email and mobile) and explains how these reduce resolution time, improving customer satisfaction and business value. She ALIGNS with Business representatives to redesign the workflow and with IT to configure OOTB features, introducing automated, skill-based work allocation and SLA logic/notifications for faster resolution.

Common Responsibilities Across All Projects

Facilitating requirement-gathering meetings

Organize and facilitate focused meetings with stakeholders and SMEs to gather and document business processes and requirements, guiding stakeholder understanding by translating how OOTB Pega features meet business requirements.

Documenting requirements as user stories

Document business requirements as user stories using Blueprint, App Studio's Agile workbench, and other Scrum tools. Pega developers (System Architects) use those user stories to configure the application to make the best use of Pega's strengths.

Demonstrating OOTB functionality

Use Blueprint to quickly redesign a workflow based on OOTB features and case-management best practices (building stakeholder alignment), and use App Studio to demonstrate how additional Pega features address requirements.

Promoting Pega best practices

Across the project landscape — organizational structure, user-story and backlog creation, governance, and testing — the BA follows and instructs on Pega's proven best practices throughout design and delivery.

Core Delivery Team Stakeholders

Product Owner (PO)

A client Business-team representative primarily responsible for managing project SCOPE and PRIORITIZING requirements throughout development. The BA runs user-story review meetings with the PO to finalize acceptance criteria and prioritize the backlog so desired features land in the timeline.

Project Delivery Lead (PDL)

The project manager who leads the Pega delivery team, maintains governance, and liaises with client leadership on timeline, budget, and scope — the BA works with the PDL on governance decisions.

Scrum Master

Promotes and supports Scrum; organizes the team, facilitates ceremonies (Sprint Planning, Sizing, Backlog Refinement, Retrospective), and is accountable for team success and removing blockers.

System Architects (SA / SSA / LSA)

The Pega developers (SA, SSA, LSA) who configure the application. The LSA imports the Blueprint and configures requirements from user stories; BAs translate technical information for stakeholders.

Quality Analysts (QAs)

Ensure functionality meets documented requirements. QAs build test scripts from user stories, participate in sizing, and flag defects. The BA helps decide if a flagged item is a defect or a new requirement.

Business-Side and Specialist Stakeholders

Subject Matter Experts (SMEs)

Client representatives with first-hand knowledge of the business process steps. BAs collaborate with SMEs in an operational walkthrough to observe the as-is process and capture all work, in and out of the system.

Business Analysts

Client representatives primarily responsible for documenting the current 'as-is' process, data requirements, and business requirements. The BA reviews this documentation to identify redundancies and bottlenecks, recommend automations, determine resource needs, and define data/interface details.

UX Designers

Ensure the design meets end-user needs, complies with accessibility standards, and delivers a consistent experience across all delivery Channels. The BA collaborates to align the UI with UX requirements, maximize OOTB features, and meet business objectives.

Solutions Consultant

Works with the client during the initial sales phase, often designing the Blueprint with stakeholders, then transitions the Blueprint to the delivery BAs and System Architects before the project starts.

Parallel Development: Workspaces & Branches

Workspace

A secure, private space for one operator to build and manage features in one application independently. Changes stay private until shared to a Branch, preventing rule conflicts during parallel development.

Branch

A Ruleset container for rapidly changing records, letting teams develop features in isolation. One Branch per feature is the standard strategy; changes are only shared once stable, conflict-free, and approved.

Blueprint import → Branch (except Access Roles)

When importing a Blueprint into Pega Platform, all assets are created in a Branch except Access Roles, which are handled separately outside the Branch.

Workspace vs. Branch: a Workspace is your private sketchpad — only you see it; a Branch is the shared drafting table where a feature's pages are assembled with the team before being bound into the final book (the merged Ruleset).

Key Skills and Knowledge

Analytical & problem-solving skills

Break down complex business processes and identify opportunities for improvement and simplification.

Process design knowledge

Process design skills and knowledge of business process design to innovate new processes and ways of working that achieve the outcome.

Systems design, Pega technology & Blueprint knowledge

Understand Pega's architecture, out-of-the-box features, and Blueprint so business requirements and end-user needs can be mapped to the right Pega capabilities and solutions.

Active listening, communication & facilitation

Create a common language and shared understanding of both the business need and Pega's capabilities across the project team and stakeholders.

Knowledge of business change

Help the Business team embrace the new Pega solution and become advocates for it within their organization.
