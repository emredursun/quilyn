Collaboration Tools in App Studio

Pulse

Pulse provides contextual posts and replies. Developer collaboration is accessible in the studios; a portal navigation Pulse widget must be configured. Default contextual visibility is not made private by an @mention: use Private post and its recipient controls for a restricted conversation.

Tags

Tags organize related Cases and Pulse posts. Enter a #tag without spaces; the Tags widget supplies Case-context discovery.

Developer Assistant

Developer Assistant has Guidance, Knowledge, Work and Checklists tabs. Work opens Agile Workbench; it is not a substitute for acceptance review or project governance.

Pulse keeps the conversation with its work context; Tags label related discussions. Visibility and portal placement require deliberate configuration.

Agile Workbench vs. Agile Studio

Agile Workbench

Agile Workbench is accessible in App Studio and Dev Studio to capture stories, bugs and feedback alongside development. Work items can include screenshots/video and move through To Do, Doing and Done; it is distinct from the standalone Agile Studio application.

Agile Studio

A separate, COMPREHENSIVE Pega application for managing Agile/Scrum at scale — organizing backlogs, sprints, teams, releases, user stories, and bugs across projects. Business team members use it to create and organize the artifacts used to manage Scrum.

How to choose

Use Workbench for contextual capture in either studio. Use Agile Studio for broader project/backlog management. Synchronization requires configured integration; the existence of both tools alone does not establish it.

Agile Workbench is the notepad on your desk for jotting issues as you work; Agile Studio is the full project-management command center where the whole program's backlog and sprints are run.

User Story Readiness

User story

A user story states who needs what and why, with description and testable acceptance criteria. Business and technical participants contribute collectively; the BA is not the only author, the PO approves acceptance criteria, and Developers are responsible for sizing.

Readiness (Definition of Ready)

Definition of Ready is a locally agreed checklist, not a mandatory universal Scrum artifact. Apply the agreed readiness checks to business and technical stories; typical checks include:

  • Clearly written (unambiguous requirements)
  • Defined ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
  • Appropriately SIZED/estimated
  • PRIORITIZED by the Product Owner
  • Free of blocking dependencies

Readiness is preparation for planning, not proof of completion. Teams still clarify and adapt work together during a Sprint.

Maintaining the Backlog

Product backlog

The Product Backlog is the emergent, ordered source of candidate product work. It includes refined items and items needing further elaboration; it is not a fixed promise that all listed work will be built next.

Backlog maintenance / refinement

The Product Owner is accountable for backlog ordering with collaborative refinement. Balance near-term readiness and longer-term elaboration. During Sprint Planning, Developers select work with the PO considering the Sprint Goal, capacity and dependencies.

An ordered backlog is a changing menu of candidate work. The team plans an achievable Sprint rather than automatically pulling every top item.
