Collaboration Tools in App Studio

Pulse

A collaboration feed that lets team members post messages, comment, and reply IN CONTEXT (e.g., on a Case or feature), keeping conversations attached to the work and the relevant stakeholders informed.

Tags

Labels you apply to work items and posts so they can be ORGANIZED and easily FOUND/grouped later — improving discoverability and collaboration across the team.

Developer Assistant

An App Studio helper that surfaces guidance/feedback and helps team members view and track application development work as they build.

Pulse is the comment thread stuck to the work itself; Tags are the colored sticky-note labels that let you find related items instantly. Together they keep collaboration organized and in context.

Agile Workbench vs. Agile Studio

Agile Workbench

A lightweight tool built into App Studio for capturing and tracking user stories, bugs, and feedback in real time alongside Case design. Separate from the standalone Agile Studio application.

Agile Studio

A separate, COMPREHENSIVE Pega application for managing Agile/Scrum at scale — organizing backlogs, sprints, teams, releases, user stories, and bugs across projects. Business team members use it to create and organize the artifacts used to manage Scrum.

How to choose

Use Agile Workbench for quick, in-context capture/tracking inside App Studio; use Agile Studio for full, end-to-end Agile project and backlog management across the program.

Agile Workbench is the notepad on your desk for jotting issues as you work; Agile Studio is the full project-management command center where the whole program's backlog and sprints are run.

User Story Readiness

User story

A concise statement of a business requirement from the user's perspective, used by the team to build and test functionality. BAs author user stories that System Architects implement.

Readiness (Definition of Ready)

Teams agree their Definition of Ready for the project. Common checks before selecting a story for a sprint include:

  • Clearly written (unambiguous requirements)
  • Defined ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
  • Appropriately SIZED/estimated
  • PRIORITIZED by the Product Owner
  • Free of blocking dependencies

A ready user story is a fully-prepped recipe: ingredients listed (acceptance criteria), portion sized (estimate), and slotted on the menu (prioritized) — the kitchen (dev team) can cook it without stopping to ask questions.

Maintaining the Backlog

Product backlog

The prioritized list of user stories, bugs, and other work for the application. It is the single ordered source of what the team will build next, driving Sprint Planning.

Backlog maintenance / refinement

The Product Owner OWNS and PRIORITIZES the backlog; ongoing refinement keeps items clear, sized, and correctly ordered so the most valuable, ready work is at the top for upcoming sprints. BAs collaborate with the PO to refine and prioritize.

The backlog is a prioritized to-do list for the product: the Product Owner keeps the most valuable, ready items at the top, and the team pulls from the top each sprint.
