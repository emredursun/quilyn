FL-6.1.1 · Tool support · K2

Tool support

Tools support management, static analysis, design, data preparation, execution, coverage, non-functional testing, delivery and collaboration. Choose by the activity and evidence needed, not marketing labels.

Worked example: A coverage tool measures exercised structure; a load generator applies concurrent requests; a defect tracker records anomalies.

Close the notes: Match a testing need to an appropriate tool category.

FL-6.2.1 · Automation benefits and risks · K1

Automation benefits and risks

Automation can shorten repetitive work, improve repeatability and accelerate feedback. Adoption, maintenance, training, unrealistic expectations and unsuitable tool choices create costs and risks; tool purchase alone does not ensure success.

Worked example: A fast regression suite loses value when flaky checks are ignored; maintenance and result investigation remain necessary.

Close the notes: Recall two potential benefits and two risks of test automation.

  • CTFL syllabus v4.0.1, section 6.2 (FL-6.2.1)

Try first: Benefits and risks of test automation

Classify each as a benefit or a risk of test automation: (1) less repetitive manual work; (2) underestimated maintenance effort; (3) more consistent test execution; (4) relying on the tool when manual testing would be more suitable; (5) faster feedback from shorter execution times.

Show solution

Benefits: (1), (3) and (5). Risks: (2) and (4). Other risks include unrealistic expectations and dependency on the tool vendor.
